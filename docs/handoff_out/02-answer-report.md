# Absorber steps from patched reflectivity scans: answers to LSS_Reader2 hand-off 02

Generated by `tools/absorber_steps.py` in the analysing repository, from the SPEC data files of ChemMatCARS 15-ID-C, 2025-12 to 2026-10. File and run ids are anonymous (F…, R…). **Measured** = computed here; **reasoning** = argued; **user** = stated by the owner. Nothing was checked at the beamline.

## Method and two changes to the hand-off

- The hand-off's method (§2), with its script checked first: run on its own worked example (NX school, S6 to S9) it gives 0.789 ± 0.005, 0.762 ± 0.006 and 0.870 ± 0.005, as the hand-off says, and this tool reproduces those steps (measured, table below).
- **ROI column:** these files call the detector ROI `Pilatus` (200K), `Pilatus1M` or `Pil300K`, the one with counts; there is no `proi` and no background ROI counter.
- **Monitor error:** each point's error includes Monc's 0.05 % per point (user, 2026-11-18) for both scans.
- **Slit changes do not spoil a join** (user, 2026-11-18): Monc sits after S1 and normalises S0/S1 changes, and the normal Pilatus ROI is not cropped by any post-sample slit. So the hand-off's `Monc/Monp` jump flag marks an S1 change that Monc has already corrected, and every join is used. Which blades moved is read from `#P` (these files record it), and the step's offset by slit class is reported as a check.
- **Days:** these data files run over several days each, so a day here is a calendar date, not a file.
- **Series:** consecutive `hklscan`s at fixed H, K and wavelength sharing L values; scans in between (alignment) do not break a series but are recorded.

## Answer to question 1: how far from nominal, systematic or random?

- **Every usable step attenuates more than nominal (measured):** step/nominal median 0.785 (10th to 90th 0.697 to 0.945); 99 % of 963 joins that remove absorber below 1. Per unit of Δn, extra Al median **+4.45 µm per 50 µm unit** (10th to 90th +3.16 to +10.97), i.e. +8.9 % of the nominal thickness.
- Per-unit transmission factor: nominal 1.4247; measured median 1.4703 (the repository's earlier same-slit overlap analysis, XR_COUNTS.md, found 1.464).

## Systematics (deliverable 4)

Each join's step/nominal relative to the median of the same (n_a, n_b) join (types with ≥ 3 usable joins), tested against each candidate cause.

## What each foil is (the per-foil figure)

Two estimates per foil. **Single-foil joins:** joins whose two settings differ in that foil only, so the step is its transmission and its thickness is −ln(step)/μ directly (model-free). **Per-day fits:** the six-foil fit of each day with a wide prior (±200 µm, so the thick foils are not pulled to nominal), kept where the foil's posterior sd is below 20 µm. ## Answers (deliverable 5)

1. **Systematic, not random (measured).** The steps attenuate a median 21.5 % more than nominal, the same sign in 99 % of joins, and the same join type repeats from day to day to a median 0.43 % beyond its errors.
2. **Mostly relative (measured):** the extra grows with the units removed, about +3.55 µm per 50 µm unit (+7.1 %), with foil-to-foil differences on top (the pooled per-foil fit). A flat 1–2 µm per foil does not describe it.
3. **Changes come in steps, not as daily scatter (measured).** 42 → 33: per-run medians 0.752 to 0.767; 30 → 20: per-run medians 0.695 to 0.729; 26 → 20: per-run medians 0.563 to 0.947; 42 → 30: per-run medians 0.726 to 0.732; 18 → 16: per-run medians 0.921 to 0.922; 20 → 18: per-run medians 0.913 to 0.960; 39 → 32: per-run medians 0.782 to 0.783. Join types whose range stays within 2 % are stable over the whole year; the others jump between runs and, in at least one run (the 26 → 20 join in R17: 0.82 until 2026-09-26, then 0.56), within a run. That fits the user's suspicion of foil or beam position on a non-uniform foil better than a slow drift. The per-foil values per day are limited by the rank of each day's joins.
4. **Other causes (measured, section above):** slit changes (offsets by class, against the user's statement that none should matter); count rate, background, drift and harmonics as their slopes.

## A static calibration or patching? (deliverable 6)

A static per-join-type calibration (the median step/nominal of each recurring (n_a, n_b)) would reproduce a day's step to the across-day spread beyond errors, median 0.43 % (90th percentile 23.27 %). That is not better than 1 % for every join type, so patching remains necessary where it is possible; a static calibration is good to the stated spread elsewhere. A per-foil calibration is less well founded than a per-join one: the per-foil fits are rank-limited on most days.
