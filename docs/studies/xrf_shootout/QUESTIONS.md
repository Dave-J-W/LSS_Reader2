# XRF shootout: the questions this work answers

Plain-language questions, each with its short answer. The numbers and
figures behind each answer are in `summary.md`; every table is in
`tables.md`.

Status:
- **answered**: measured on synthetic data with known truth;
- **partly**: measured, with a stated limit;
- **open**: not measured.

Q1–Q12 are answered on synthetic spectra, Q13 on the owner's real blanks with synthetic lines added, and Q14 on real lines in real scatter (the private burn series, summary values only). Q15 and Q16 test the synthetic spectra themselves (the models study, `docs/studies/xrf_models/`). All measured 2026-11-25.

| # | Question | Status | Short answer | Where |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | What do other people use to get line intensities out of XRF spectra, and how do their methods differ from ours? | answered (reading) | PyMca, PyXRF (scikit-beam), Larch and XRF-Maps fit the **whole spectrum**, scatter peaks included. XeRay (the Lee group's MATLAB tool for XFNTR) fits a line window, as the reader does, but unweighted on a straight line. The reader fits a **line window** around the line by Poisson likelihood. | `survey.md` |
| Q2 | When a weak line sits on top of beam scatter, which method gives the right number of counts? | answered | **Under a 10 keV beam none does** for Zn Kα, including on the owner's measured detector. The reader's fits refuse (54–100 % fail their checks); PyMca answers about −20 %, PyXRF −100 %, Larch and XeRay worse. With the beam at 11–12 keV the reader is within 1 % (Poisson fit 0.0 % and −0.2 % on the measured detector). PyMca is accurate there only with the right background setting, which needs the truth to choose. Over 43 conditions: Poisson fit + fixed width + escapes removed accurate in 13, Poisson fit in 11, XeRay 11, PyMca 7 (10 if it may pick its setting with the truth), Larch 4–5, PyXRF 4. | summary §1, §8; X1, X5 |
| Q3 | Which method reproduces the true shape of an XFNTR curve (intensity against Qz)? | answered | **The reader**: best in 6 of 8 curves, tied in 1. XeRay is better on the Er L curve (1.0 % against 1.7 %). Every PyMca setting puts a 6–12 % step at the critical angle, where the bulk scatter rises. The worst point in the hard 10 keV curve: Poisson fit + fixed width + escapes removed 1.7 %, PyMca 12 %, Larch 20 %, PyXRF 41 %. | summary §2; X3 |
| Q4 | Are each method's error bars honest, so that a χ² fit downstream means something? | answered | **Only the reader's fits'** (pull SD 1.03 for Poisson fit + fixed width + escapes removed and 1.07 for Poisson fit). PyMca's are 1.3–2× too small under scatter. XeRay's (√counts) are up to 11× too small. Larch's are too large. PyXRF reports none. | summary §3; X4 |
| Q5 | When counts are low (the high-Qz end of a curve), which methods stay unbiased? | answered | Poisson fit + fixed width + escapes removed is within 2 % down to 30 photons; Poisson fit from 60 photons (+23 % at 30). The old data weights read −23 % at 100 photons; PyMca (also data weights) reads +16 % at 60. On a clean line the others are 10–25 % **more precise** (they also use Kβ and fit a simpler background): this is the reader's one plain weakness. | summary §4; X2 |
| Q6 | When two elements' lines overlap, does a fit of a small line window hold up against a fit of the whole spectrum? | answered | **No.** A whole-spectrum fit wins when a neighbour's line falls in or beside the line window: Ga beside Zn, PyMca −1.1 % against the reader failing; Co beside Fe, PyMca +2.8 % against the reader's +53 %. An exact overlap (As Kα under Pb Lα) and Zn+Cu under the 10 keV scatter defeat everyone. | summary §5 |
| Q7 | How much does each method suffer from a slightly wrong energy calibration or detector model? | partly | It was tested only on the 10 keV hard case, where every method already fails, so it does not separate them (a limit of the design: rerun at 12 keV). One clear result: the reader's fixed-width option (fixed width) assumes Fano 0.115. On the measured detector (Fano 0.137) it reads −5.5 %, where Poisson fit alone reads −0.2 %. | summary §6 |
| Q8 | Does the verdict depend on how we simulated the Compton scatter (our approximation against tabulated Hartree–Fock profiles)? | answered | **No.** Tabulated (Biggs) profiles and study B's shell model agree within 1 % under the Zn line. The reader's and PyMca's results move by under a point between them (Poisson fit + fixed width + escapes removed −7.6 against −7.9 %, PyMca −20.6 against −19.8 %). Larch swings from +34 % to −4 %, which is its own instability. The total Compton scatter matches xraylib's incoherent scattering function within 1.2 %. | summary §7; X7; `tests/test_mca_more.py` |
| Q9 | Are the "from memory" physics constants in our synthetic data right, checked against a tabulated library? | answered | Line energies and edges match xraylib to 0.01 %. The Si escape constants are within 1–4 %. The O 1s binding is 0.543 against xraylib's 0.532 keV. **Kβ/Kα in `mca.ELEMENTS` is 16–28 % low** for Fe to Br; this affects the synthetic data only. | `physics_check.csv`; X7 |
| Q10 | How fast is each method? | answered | Per spectrum, one CPU: window 0.1 ms, Poisson fit 4 ms, Poisson fit + fixed width + escapes removed 8 ms, XeRay 13 ms, PyMca 25 ms, Larch (series) 0.14 s, PyXRF 0.18 s, Larch (each) 1.2 s. | summary §9; X8 |
| Q11 | Where are we worse than others, and what would it take to fix it? | answered (fixes not built) | On real scatter (Q13), turn the escape removal on: it is what makes the reader the most accurate method run blind. Ti K and La L at 6.1 keV remain behind PyMca's best setting. On synthetic spectra we are worse in two places. (1) **Precision on isolated lines**: fit Kα+Kβ together and offer a linear background. (2) **Overlapping lines**: place the known neighbours' lines in the line window (study B's `fit_known`, made an option). Also take fixed width's width from the detector's measured Fano and noise. | summary, "What would close the gaps" |
| Q13 | Does the verdict hold on real scatter: the owner's measured blanks, with synthetic lines of known photons added? | answered | **Yes, and the reader does better there.** Over 192 conditions on 10 real blanks (10, 9.2 and 6.1 keV; Zn, Cu, Ni, Cr, Ti K and La L), Poisson fit + escapes removed (escape strip) is accurate in 49 and Poisson fit + fixed width + escapes removed in 47. The best fixed PyMca setting manages 20, PyXRF 15, XeRay 6 and Larch 1; PyMca reaches 56 only when its setting is picked with the truth. The real scatter carries a **Si escape peak 1.74 keV below the beam**, which Poisson fit alone reads as a line (Ni under 9.2 keV +22 %). The strip removes it. Error bars: the reader's are honest (pull SD 0.98–1.08), PyMca's are not (1.56–1.87). The reader still loses on Ti K and La L at 6.1 keV. | summary §10; X9–X11; `blanks_tables.md` |
| Q14 | On real lines in real scatter, which method is unbiased at low counts, which has honest error bars, and do they agree? | answered (no absolute truth) | On 19 real burn series (the single-element detector, a line near 8 keV, 27–4500 counts per spectrum), tested against resampled twins with the series' own shape. **Poisson fit + fixed width + escapes removed has the smallest low-count bias** (−2 to −7.5 % at 27–58 counts, against −6 to −25 % for Poisson fit and −12 to +13 % for PyMca), and **honest error bars in every series** (pull SD 0.95–1.11). At high counts the reader is within 0.4 % and PyMca reads 2.4–6 % low. The methods disagree by ±11 % on the sums: the real line is 21 % wider than the detector's measured resolution, so fixed width's fixed width reads 11 % low there. **Why:** the burn series were recorded in another setup of the same detector, where real lines are 1.19–1.24 × the resolution measured in the blanks' setup (Q16). | summary §11; X12; `real_tables.md` |
| Q12 | Is Zn under a 10 keV beam measurable by anyone? | answered | **Not when the line is weak** (0.05 of the scatter under it): no method is within 2 %, on study B's hard case or on the measured detector. Choose the beam energy instead: at 12 keV the reader's default is accurate. On the single-element detector's real blanks, once the line is as strong as the background in its line window, Poisson fit + escapes removed is within 0.2 % (Q13). | summary §1, §8 |
| Q15 | Can a model stand in for the real excitation scatter under a line, or must tests use the real blanks? | answered | **Only partly, and only the full model.** Run through the C66 shootout, a fit's bias on scatter-only model twins of the blanks is a median 9–11 % from its bias on the real blank for the generator's defaults and a Gaussian detector, and 4.0 % for the full model (tails, shelf, high tail free), against a floor of 2.1 % between two draws of one truth. The full model is within 1.3–3.7 × the floor for Cr, Cu, Ni and Zn, but not for Ti K and La L under 6.1 keV (8–9 % against 3–4 %), where its twins flatter Poisson fit + fixed width + escapes removed (17–22 of 64 accurate against 4). Keep real blanks wherever one exists. | `docs/studies/xrf_models/README.md` part 1; M1–M3 |
| Q16 | Is a Gaussian enough for real fluorescence lines, on the single-element and the 7-element detector? | answered | **The single-element detector: nearly**, about 5 % outside a Gaussian, but the width is its setup's (1.03 × or 1.19–1.24 × the resolution measured on the scatter). **The 7-element detector (one element of it): no**, 23–81 % of each line (median 46 %) is in a 48–87 eV low-energy skew, per element. In the shootout a Gaussian line gives the same verdicts as the elastic-shaped one; the real B shape costs Poisson fit + fixed width + escapes removed 6 % and leaves Poisson fit + escapes removed within 2 % except on La L. | `docs/studies/xrf_models/README.md` part 2; M4, M5 |
